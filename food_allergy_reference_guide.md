# The Complete Food Allergy Reference Guide

## Before the list: one honest fact

There is no closed, finite list of "all food allergies" — **over 170 individual foods have been medically documented** as causing allergic reactions in at least someone, somewhere. Any food containing protein can theoretically trigger an allergy in a susceptible person. What regulators actually do is maintain a *shorter* "priority" or "major" list — the foods responsible for the overwhelming majority (≈90%) of serious reactions — because that's the list that's actually enforceable and useful on a label.

So this guide has three layers:
1. **The regulated "major allergens"** — different by country, all listed below
2. **The wider documented list** — the other 150+ foods, organized by category
3. **Related conditions** that get confused with allergy but work differently (celiac disease, FPIES, pollen-food cross-reactivity, alpha-gal syndrome) — worth knowing because they're often mistaken for "just" a food allergy

## 1. The regulated "major allergen" lists, by country

No two countries use exactly the same list. If you're checking a label, check for the country the product was made in.

| Country/Region | # | Full list |
|---|---|---|
| **USA (FDA)** | 9 | Milk, eggs, fish, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, sesame |
| **EU / UK** | 14 | Cereals containing gluten (wheat, rye, barley, oats), crustaceans, eggs, fish, peanuts, soybeans, milk, tree nuts, celery, mustard, sesame, sulphur dioxide/sulphites (>10mg/kg), lupin, molluscs |
| **Canada** | 11 + sulphites | Milk, eggs, mustard, peanuts, crustaceans & molluscs, fish, sesame, soy, tree nuts, wheat/triticale, sulphites |
| **Australia / New Zealand** | ~12 | Cereals with gluten, crustaceans, eggs, fish, milk, peanuts, soybeans, tree nuts, sesame, lupins, added sulfites >10mg/kg, plus royal jelly/bee pollen/propolis (warning label required, not full allergen status) |
| **Japan** | 8 mandatory (+ 20 recommended) | Crustaceans, egg, milk, peanut, tree nuts (walnut recently added), wheat, buckwheat, and (separately tracked) shrimp/crab |
| **South Korea** | Big-8 + extras | Adds buckwheat, molluscan shellfish, beef, chicken, pork, peach, tomato on top of the international base list |
| **India (FSSAI)** | 8 | Cereals containing gluten, crustaceans, milk & milk products, eggs, fish, peanuts & tree nuts (declared jointly as "Nut"), soybeans, sulphites ≥10mg/kg |

A few notable quirks:
- **Coconut** is legally classified as a tree nut in the US (though botanically a fruit) — but as of the FDA's January 2025 guidance, the FDA now names only 12 specific tree nuts as "major" (almond, walnut, cashew, hazelnut, macadamia, pecan, pistachio, pine nut, Brazil nut, and a few regional walnut/heartnut variants) — coconut and a handful of others were dropped from that specific list, though this is a narrow technical point and doesn't mean coconut allergy isn't real.
- **Mango, peach, tomato, buckwheat, beef, chicken, pork** appear on East Asian mandatory/recommended lists but nowhere in the US/EU list — a reminder that "major allergen" is a regional, evidence-and-prevalence-based category, not a universal biological one.

## 2. What's inside each major category

| Category | What it includes |
|---|---|
| **Milk** | Cow's milk proteins (casein, whey). Cross-reacts often with goat/sheep/buffalo milk — FDA "milk" legally means cow only, so check separately if avoiding all mammalian milk |
| **Eggs** | Chicken egg (white and yolk); cross-reacts with duck, goose, quail egg |
| **Fish** | Any finned fish (cod, salmon, tuna, etc.) — regulators require the specific species be named |
| **Crustacean shellfish** | Shrimp, crab, lobster, crayfish — high cross-reactivity within this group (tropomyosin protein) |
| **Molluscs** (EU/UK/Canada/Aus-NZ, not US-mandatory) | Clams, oysters, scallops, mussels, squid, snails — lower cross-reactivity with crustaceans than once thought, but common to react to both |
| **Tree nuts** | Almond, walnut, cashew, pistachio, pecan, hazelnut, Brazil nut, macadamia, pine nut, chestnut |
| **Peanuts** | A legume, not a tree nut — but shares a labeling category due to severity/prevalence |
| **Wheat / gluten cereals** | Wheat, rye, barley, oats (and their hybrids like triticale) — wheat allergy is distinct from celiac disease and from gluten intolerance |
| **Soybeans** | Includes soy lecithin, tofu, edamame, miso, soy sauce |
| **Sesame** | Includes tahini, sesame oil — allergy has risen sharply in the last two decades |
| **Celery** (EU/UK/Aus-NZ) | Includes celeriac, celery salt, celery seed — often hidden in stocks/seasoning |
| **Mustard** (EU/UK/Canada) | Seeds, leaves, and mustard-derived condiments |
| **Lupin** (EU/UK/Aus-NZ) | A legume related to peanut/soy, increasingly used as a flour in gluten-free baking |
| **Sulphites** | Not a protein allergen in the classic sense — a chemical sensitivity, but regulated alongside allergens because reactions can be severe, especially in people with asthma |

## 3. The wider documented list (beyond the "majors")

These are real, medically reported food allergies — just less common, so they're not on mandatory labels in most countries.

**Fruits:** kiwi, banana, avocado, mango, papaya, pineapple, apple, pear, peach, plum, apricot, cherry, strawberry, grape, melon (cantaloupe, honeydew, watermelon), fig, pomegranate, citrus fruits, lychee

**Vegetables:** tomato, potato, sweet potato, carrot, corn/maize, garlic, onion, bell pepper/capsicum, cucumber, zucchini, eggplant/brinjal, cabbage, cauliflower, broccoli

**Legumes (beyond peanut/soy/lupin):** lentils, chickpeas, green peas, kidney beans, black-eyed peas — worth flagging for South Asian diets, where legume allergy is more prevalent than in Western populations

**Grains & pseudo-grains (beyond wheat/gluten):** rice, corn, buckwheat, quinoa, amaranth, teff, millet

**Seeds (beyond sesame):** sunflower seed, poppy seed, hemp seed, chia, flax

**Meats & poultry:** beef, pork, chicken, lamb, mutton, and — as a distinct tick-triggered syndrome — red meat generally (see Alpha-gal, below)

**Spices & herbs:** cinnamon, coriander/cilantro, cumin, fennel, caraway, anise, black pepper, garlic, mustard (also a major allergen), vanilla, paprika

**Other:** gelatin, royal jelly, bee pollen, propolis, yeast, chocolate/cocoa, coffee, alcohol-derived proteins in beer/wine

## 4. Cross-reactivity syndromes (not the same as a direct food allergy)

### Pollen-Food Allergy Syndrome (PFAS) / Oral Allergy Syndrome (OAS)
If you have hay fever, your immune system may also react to raw fruits/veg/nuts whose proteins resemble the pollen protein it already recognizes. Usually mild (itchy mouth, lips, throat), and cooking or peeling the food usually destroys the trigger protein — so a baked apple pie is fine even if a raw apple isn't. Grouped by the pollen you're sensitive to:

| Pollen | Cross-reactive foods |
|---|---|
| **Birch** (spring) | Apple, pear, peach, plum, apricot, cherry, kiwi, carrot, celery, parsley, almond, hazelnut, soybean, peanut |
| **Grass** (summer) | Tomato, melon, orange, peach, white potato |
| **Ragweed** (late summer/fall) | Banana, melon (cantaloupe/honeydew/watermelon), cucumber, zucchini, sunflower seed |
| **Mugwort** (fall) | Celery, carrot, fennel, coriander, parsley, sunflower, bell pepper, cabbage family |

### Latex-Fruit Syndrome
People with a natural rubber latex allergy often react to structurally similar plant proteins in **banana, avocado, kiwi, and chestnut**.

### Alpha-gal Syndrome (red meat allergy from a tick bite)
Unlike a normal food allergy, this is *acquired* — a bite from a lone star tick (mainly in the US) transfers a sugar molecule (galactose-α-1,3-galactose, "alpha-gal") into the bloodstream, causing some people to develop a delayed (3–8 hour) allergic reaction to **red meat (beef, pork, lamb, venison)**, and sometimes **dairy and gelatin**. Poultry and fish are unaffected because birds and fish don't carry alpha-gal. It doesn't resolve on its own the way many childhood allergies do.

## 5. Related conditions — often mistaken for "just an allergy," but mechanically different

| Condition | What it actually is | Common triggers |
|---|---|---|
| **Celiac disease** | Autoimmune, not allergic — gluten triggers an immune attack on the small intestine | Wheat, barley, rye (gluten) |
| **FPIES** (Food Protein-Induced Enterocolitis Syndrome) | Non-IgE, delayed (1–4 hr) gut reaction — mainly infants; no skin/breathing symptoms, just severe vomiting/diarrhea | Cow's milk, soy, rice, oats most common; fish and shellfish are the leading adult-onset trigger |
| **Eosinophilic esophagitis** | Chronic immune/inflammatory reaction in the esophagus | Milk, egg, wheat, soy most commonly implicated |
| **Histamine intolerance** | Not an immune reaction — an enzyme (DAO) deficiency in breaking down dietary histamine | Aged cheese, cured meats, fermented foods, wine, some fish |
| **Sulfite sensitivity** | Chemical, not protein-based; worse in people with asthma | Wine, dried fruit, processed potatoes |
| **Lactose intolerance** | Not an allergy — a missing digestive enzyme (lactase), not immune-mediated at all | Milk and dairy (the sugar lactose, not the protein) |

## The honest summary

- If you want the **legally enforceable, label-worthy list**, use Section 1 for whichever country you're in.
- If you want the **realistic full picture**, treat Section 3 as a starting point, not an ending one — genuinely any food protein can be an allergen for someone.
- If a reaction doesn't fit the "hives within minutes" pattern, it might be one of the conditions in Section 5 rather than a classic allergy — worth mentioning to a doctor specifically, since the tests and management are different.
- A confirmed diagnosis always requires an allergist — this guide is for orientation, not self-diagnosis.
