# The Complete Nutrient Reference Guide
### Categories, sources, and safety limits — for packaged food and natural food alike

---

## How to read this document

There is no single scale on which every nutrient is "safe" or "unsafe" per 100g. Three different systems apply, depending on what kind of nutrient it is:

| Nutrient type | Limit unit | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Vitamins & minerals | **UL — per day** (not per 100g) | Vitamin A UL = 3,000 mcg/day |
| Food additives (preservatives, sweeteners, colors) | **ADI — per kg body weight, per day** | Aspartame ADI ≈ 40 mg/kg bw/day |
| Contaminants (heavy metals, mycotoxins, pesticide residue) | **Max level — per kg of food**, varies by food category | Lead in baby food: 0.01–0.02 mg/kg; in spices: up to 3 mg/kg |
| Fat, saturated fat, sugar, salt (macronutrients) | **Genuinely per 100g** — this is the one true "per-100g" system, used on food labels | Sugar: "high" = >22.5g/100g |

Where a per-100g number is asked for but doesn't exist in regulation (true for almost all vitamins, minerals, and additives), I've given the real unit instead of forcing a fake one.

## 1. Macronutrients (needed in grams per day)

| Nutrient | Function | Main food sources | Per-100g "high" threshold (UK/EU front-of-pack standard) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | Primary energy source | Grains, fruit, legumes, starchy vegetables | No universal high/low threshold |
| Protein | Tissue building/repair, enzymes | Meat, fish, eggs, dairy, legumes, soy | "High protein" claim = ≥20% of calories from protein |
| Total fat | Energy storage, cell membranes, vitamin absorption | Oils, nuts, dairy, meat | >17.5 g/100g = high; ≤3 g/100g = low |
| Saturated fat | Energy, but raises LDL cholesterol in excess | Butter, coconut oil, red meat, cheese | >5 g/100g = high; ≤1.5 g/100g = low |
| Trans fat | Industrially hydrogenated — no known safe level | Partially hydrogenated oils, some fried/baked packaged goods | WHO recommends **elimination**; many countries cap industrial trans fat at 2g/100g of total fat in the product |
| Dietary fiber | Digestion, satiety, gut microbiome | Whole grains, legumes, fruit, vegetables | "High fiber" claim = ≥6g/100g (EU) |
| Sugars (total) | Quick energy | Fruit, dairy (lactose), added sugar, honey | >22.5 g/100g = high; ≤5 g/100g = low |
| Water | Solvent, temperature regulation, transport | All food and drink | Not applicable (toxicity only from acute overconsumption — "water intoxication" — a separate, rare medical emergency, not a nutrient-limit issue) |

## 2. Vitamins

UL = Tolerable Upper Intake Level for a healthy adult, **per day** (US NASEM / EU EFSA figures — broadly similar, noted where they diverge). "ND" = No UL established, usually because there isn't enough data to define one safely — **not** because unlimited intake is fine.

| Vitamin | Adult UL/day | Risk if chronically exceeded | Good sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (retinol) | 3,000 mcg RAE | Liver damage, birth defects, bone thinning | Liver, dairy, egg yolk, orange/leafy vegetables (as beta-carotene, which is much safer) |
| D | 100 mcg (4,000 IU) | Hypercalcemia, kidney damage | Sunlight, fatty fish, fortified dairy |
| E | 1,000 mg (supplemental) | Increased bleeding risk | Nuts, seeds, vegetable oils |
| K | ND | No established toxicity from food/supplements | Leafy greens, vegetable oils |
| C | 2,000 mg | GI upset, kidney stones (high-risk individuals) | Citrus, peppers, berries |
| B1 (Thiamin) | ND | None established | Whole grains, pork, legumes |
| B2 (Riboflavin) | ND | None established | Dairy, eggs, leafy greens |
| B3 (Niacin) | 35 mg (supplemental/fortified) | Flushing, liver toxicity | Meat, fish, whole grains |
| B5 (Pantothenic acid) | ND | None established | Meat, avocado, whole grains |
| B6 | 100 mg | Nerve damage (reversible) | Poultry, fish, potatoes, bananas |
| B7 (Biotin) | ND | None established | Eggs, nuts, seeds |
| B9 (Folate/folic acid) | 1,000 mcg (synthetic form only) | Can mask B12 deficiency | Leafy greens, legumes, fortified grains |
| B12 | ND | None established | Meat, fish, dairy, eggs (absent in plant foods) |
| Choline (vitamin-like) | 3,500 mg | Fishy body odor, low blood pressure, liver toxicity | Eggs, liver, soybeans |

## 3. Minerals

| Mineral | Adult UL/day | Risk if chronically exceeded | Good sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| Calcium | 2,500 mg (19–50 yrs); 2,000 mg (51+) | Kidney stones, impaired iron/zinc absorption | Dairy, fortified plant milk, leafy greens |
| Phosphorus | 4,000 mg | Impaired calcium absorption | Dairy, meat, fish, legumes |
| Magnesium | 350 mg (from supplements only; no UL from food) | Diarrhea, cramping | Nuts, seeds, whole grains, leafy greens |
| Iron | 45 mg | GI distress; organ damage over long term | Red meat, legumes, spinach, fortified cereal |
| Zinc | 40 mg | Copper deficiency, immune suppression | Meat, shellfish, legumes, seeds |
| Copper | 10,000 mcg (10 mg) | Liver damage | Shellfish, nuts, seeds, organ meats |
| Manganese | 11 mg | Neurological effects (rare, from food) | Whole grains, nuts, tea |
| Selenium | 400 mcg | Hair/nail loss, GI upset, neurological symptoms | Brazil nuts, fish, eggs |
| Iodine | 1,100 mcg | Thyroid dysfunction | Iodized salt, seafood, dairy |
| Molybdenum | 2,000 mcg | Rare — reproductive effects in animal studies | Legumes, grains, nuts |
| Fluoride | 10 mg | Skeletal fluorosis | Fluoridated water, tea |
| Chromium | ND | None established | Broccoli, whole grains, meat |
| Potassium | No UL from food in healthy people (AI is roughly 2,600–3,400 mg/day depending on sex/region) | Dangerous only with impaired kidney function | Bananas, potatoes, legumes, leafy greens |
| Sodium | CDRR (Chronic Disease Risk Reduction) = 2,300 mg | Hypertension, cardiovascular risk | Table salt, processed/packaged food, sauces |
| Chloride | ~3,600 mg (paired with sodium CDRR) | Rarely a practical concern from food | Table salt |

## 4. Other essential dietary components

| Component | Notes on safety |
|---|---|
| Essential fatty acids (omega-3 ALA/EPA/DHA, omega-6 linoleic acid) | No UL established; high-dose fish oil supplements (3g+/day EPA+DHA) can increase bleeding risk — a supplement-dose issue, not a food one |
| Essential amino acids (9 total: histidine, isoleucine, leucine, lysine, methionine, phenylalanine, threonine, tryptophan, valine) | No ULs from whole food; excess from very high-dose isolated amino acid supplements can stress kidneys/liver |
| Cholesterol | No UL currently set (2015 US guidelines removed the old 300mg/day cap); individual response varies — those with familial hypercholesterolemia should follow personalized medical advice |
| Alcohol (ethanol) | Not a nutrient — no risk-free level per WHO. Older moderate-drinking guidance (up to ~1 drink/day women, ~2 men) has been walked back by many health bodies in recent years |
| Caffeine | ~400 mg/day is the amount the US FDA associates with no adverse effects in most healthy adults (roughly 4 cups of coffee). Acute toxicity risk begins in the multi-gram range from concentrated powder/supplements — a very different scenario from drinking coffee |

## 5. Bioactive plant compounds (phytochemicals) — no established "unsafe" limits

These occur naturally and are generally protective, not toxic, at normal dietary intakes. No regulatory ULs exist for whole-food sources.

- **Polyphenols** (flavonoids, anthocyanins, catechins) — tea, berries, cocoa, red wine
- **Carotenoids** (lycopene, lutein, zeaxanthin, beta-carotene) — tomatoes, leafy greens, carrots
- **Glucosinolates** — cruciferous vegetables (broccoli, cabbage) — can affect thyroid function only at very high, sustained raw intakes in iodine-deficient people
- **Phytosterols** — vegetable oils, nuts, fortified spreads
- **Tannins** — tea, coffee, red wine — can reduce iron absorption if consumed with iron-rich meals

## 6. Food additives

Regulated by ADI (mg per **kg of your body weight**, per **day** — not per 100g of the food). Figures below are internationally recognized JECFA/EFSA/FDA reference points; exact numbers can differ slightly by regulator, and India's FSSAI Sixth Schedule is the authority for exact permitted limits in packaged food sold in India.

| Additive | Typical use | Approx. ADI (mg/kg body weight/day) |
|---|---|---|
| Aspartame | Sweetener | ~40 (EU) / ~50 (US) |
| Sucralose | Sweetener | ~5 (US) / ~15 (EU) |
| Acesulfame-K | Sweetener | ~15 |
| Saccharin | Sweetener | ~5 |
| Sodium benzoate | Preservative | ~5 |
| Sodium/potassium sorbate | Preservative | ~25 |
| Sodium nitrite | Cured-meat preservative | ~0.07 (also has a direct food-concentration cap, roughly 100–150 mg/kg of finished product in the EU) |
| Sulfites (as SO₂) | Preservative, wine/dried fruit | ~0.7 |
| MSG (monosodium glutamate) | Flavor enhancer | "Not specified" — considered self-limiting by taste, classified GRAS/JECFA-acceptable |

## 7. Contaminants — never "safe" as such, only "below the legal maximum"

Unlike nutrients, these are never intentionally added and there's no "beneficial" amount — regulation exists purely to cap exposure. Limits are set per **kg of food** and vary enormously by category (this is one of the few places a true "per weight of food" number exists, though it's per kg, i.e. divide by 10 for per-100g).

| Contaminant | Typical EU maximum level | Highest-risk foods |
|---|---|---|
| Lead | 0.01–3 mg/kg (strictest for baby food, loosest for supplements) | Rice, leafy vegetables, offal |
| Cadmium | 0.005–3 mg/kg | Shellfish, offal, cacao, leafy vegetables |
| Mercury | 0.1–1 mg/kg (species-dependent) | Large predatory fish: swordfish, shark, tuna |
| Inorganic arsenic | 0.01–0.5 mg/kg | Rice and rice products |
| Aflatoxins (mycotoxin) | Typically 2–15 μg/kg depending on food/nut type | Peanuts, corn, tree nuts, spices stored in humid conditions |
| Acrylamide | Indicative benchmark levels, not hard legal limits, e.g. ~500 μg/kg for potato chips | Fried/baked starchy foods (chips, crackers, coffee) |

## 8. The one genuine "per-100g" cheat sheet (UK/EU front-of-pack system)

This is the real, standardized answer to "safe vs. not safe per 100g" — it just only covers four things:

| Nutrient (per 100g, solid food) | 🟢 Low | 🟡 Medium | 🔴 High |
|---|---|---|---|
| Total fat | ≤3.0 g | 3.0–17.5 g | >17.5 g |
| Saturated fat | ≤1.5 g | 1.5–5.0 g | >5.0 g |
| Total sugars | ≤5.0 g | 5.0–22.5 g | >22.5 g |
| Salt | ≤0.3 g | 0.3–1.5 g | >1.5 g |

(Drinks use lower thresholds since you consume them in larger volumes.)

---

## The honest summary

- **Macronutrients & the four labeled nutrients above** → yes, real per-100g thresholds exist.
- **Vitamins & minerals** → "unsafe" is a per-day total-intake question (food + supplements + fortified products combined), not a per-100g-of-one-food question. A food is essentially never "unsafe" for its vitamin/mineral content alone unless you're eating enormous quantities or stacking supplements on top.
- **Additives** → governed by your body weight and daily total exposure across all foods, not by concentration in a single item.
- **Contaminants** → the only category with a true "don't exceed this concentration" rule, and it's per kg, food-category-specific, and enforced at the regulatory/import level rather than something you calculate yourself.
